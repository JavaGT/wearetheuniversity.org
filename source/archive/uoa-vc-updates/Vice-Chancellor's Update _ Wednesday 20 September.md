Kia ora .,

Yesterday we welcomed the Prime Minister, other Government Ministers and
representatives of Ngāti Whātua Ōrākei for the official opening of the newly
refurbished Building B201.

The building is a triumph of clever design, adaptation and sustainability,
achieving a massive reduction in carbon emissions compared to a new-build
project, and a 6 Green Star certification from the New Zealand Green Building
Council, the highest possible.

It will become home to three faculties

 * Education and Social Work (EDSW)
 * Arts
 * Creative Arts and Industries (CAI)

You can read more about the building and opening ceremony on our News pages, as
well as a poem dedicated to the building by Professor Selina Tusitala Marsh.

From Epsom to the City

The opening of B201 is a major milestone in EDSW's preparations to move from its
current location in Epsom to the main City Campus. The move will begin in
November and continue through to the start of teaching in Semester One 2024. We
look forward to welcoming our EDSW students and staff to their new home.

Spring Graduation

This year's Spring Graduation truly lived up to its name, with glorious warm
sunny weather and flowers everywhere. Congratulations to the 2,062 students who
graduated in person this week, with a further 1,176 in absentia. Yesterday's
ceremonies marked 130 years to the day since women in Aotearoa New Zealand won
the right to vote; so it was particularly apt that, as part of the University's
140th anniversary, we conferred a posthumous honorary doctorate on Kate Edgar,
the first woman in this country to gain a university degree.
